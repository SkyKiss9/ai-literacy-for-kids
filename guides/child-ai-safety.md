# Child AI Safety Guide

This guide is for teachers, parents, and volunteers introducing AI to elementary school students.

## Core Rules For Children

1. Do not share private information with AI.
2. Do not believe every AI answer.
3. Ask an adult before using AI for something serious.
4. Use AI to learn, not to copy.
5. Be kind. Do not use AI to hurt, trick, or embarrass others.
6. Check important answers with books, trusted websites, teachers, parents, or real-world observation.

## What Children Should Not Enter Into AI

- Full name
- Address
- Phone number
- Passwords
- School name and class schedule
- Photos of themselves or classmates
- Family financial details
- Health details
- Private worries that require adult help

## Adult Supervision

For elementary school students, AI use should be guided by an adult. The safest setup is:

- Adult controls the AI account and device
- Students suggest questions
- Adult reads and filters responses
- Class discusses whether the answer is useful and reliable

## Handling Wrong Answers

Children should learn that a wrong AI answer is not a failure. It is a chance to practice checking evidence.

Useful follow-up questions:

- How do we know?
- What source can we check?
- Can we observe it ourselves?
- What would a teacher or expert say?
- Did AI guess?

## Healthy Learning Use

Encourage AI use for:

- Asking for simpler explanations
- Generating practice questions
- Brainstorming ideas
- Improving a draft after the child writes first
- Learning vocabulary
- Comparing different viewpoints with adult guidance

Discourage AI use for:

- Copying homework
- Avoiding thinking
- Asking for secret advice without adult help
- Producing unkind messages
- Pretending AI work is the student's own work
