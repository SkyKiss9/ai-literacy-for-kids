# Teacher Guide

This guide helps teachers run AI literacy lessons with elementary school students.

## Teaching Approach

The course is built around three habits:

- Ask better questions
- Check answers
- Use AI responsibly

The goal is not to teach children to depend on AI. The goal is to help them understand AI as a tool and build good judgment early.

## Recommended Classroom Setup

For early lessons:

- Use one teacher-controlled AI account
- Project the screen only if privacy rules are clear
- Let students propose questions on paper
- Discuss AI responses together
- Do not let students enter private information

## Lesson Rhythm

Each lesson can follow this pattern:

1. Warm-up discussion
2. Teacher demonstration
3. Student question or activity
4. AI response review
5. Safety or critical-thinking moment
6. Reflection

## Discussion Questions

Use these questions often:

- What did AI do well?
- What might be wrong?
- What information is missing?
- How could we ask a clearer question?
- Where else can we check?
- Did we use AI to help us think or to avoid thinking?

## Classroom Norms

Recommended norms:

- We do not put private information into AI.
- We are allowed to question AI answers.
- We use AI to support learning, not to copy.
- We treat classmates kindly.
- We ask an adult when something feels serious, private, or confusing.

## Feedback

After each lesson, teachers can record:

- What students understood quickly
- What confused them
- Which prompts worked well
- Which safety reminders were needed
- What should be changed before the next class
